4Two states the first pass left open — a payout that bounces, and a platform that pays in two parts
Assumptions
Reversed is terminal, and it spawns a successor. A record never re-enters a stage it has left — that would break "current stage is the last event" and poison the settle-time percentiles. The re-issue is a new record linked to the old one.
A reversal usually costs money and clears nothing. Commission and withdrawal fees are already spent and are not refunded. This is the only place in the app where a record's contribution to income is negative.
Split records keep one set of hours. Settlements divide the money, never the effort. Per-settlement effective rates would be arithmetic with no meaning, so the rate is only ever shown on the parent.
Neither state is an error. Reversals happen because a bank name field disagrees; part payment is how some platforms always pay. Both get the same flat, unalarmed treatment as everything else.
Adds one stage — Reversed — and one relation: a record may own settlements, and may point at a predecessor. Both are additive to the schema in DATA_MODEL.md, so neither needs a migration later.
4aPayout reversed — the chain breaks after Received. The hours stay, the fees stay, and the successor record is one tap away.

2Technical improvements — things the data model can do that the first pass left on the table
Why these four
Sync is a screen, not a spinner. An app that is offline several hours a day needs its queue, its conflicts and its rate staleness to be inspectable. 2a
"Average days" is the wrong statistic. Settling times are long-tailed; an average flags nothing and hides everything. Overdue should mean p90. 2b
The calculator answers the wrong question. "Cheapest route for this amount" is easy; "is this amount big enough to withdraw yet" is the one that saves money. 2c
Stage changes arrive in batches. One email sweep updates five records. Tapping rows one at a time is the wrong interaction for that. 2d
Under the surface, all four assume the same thing: local-first storage with an append-only stage log. Every transition is a row with a timestamp, a source (manual, email parse, API) and an idempotency key, so a record's current stage is derived, never stored. That is what makes offline edits replayable, conflicts detectable, settle-time percentiles free to compute, and the record-detail history in 1e a read of the log rather than a separate table. Rates are snapshotted per conversion, so nothing is ever silently revalued.

Assumptions I made
